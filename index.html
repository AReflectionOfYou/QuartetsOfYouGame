      Make your own puzzles

      Repertoire

      A puzzle is a title and four quartets of four words. A pack is one or more puzzles in a single JSON file that you can put online and share. There are three ways to make one.

      1. Use the form

        Fill in the quartets, try each puzzle out, then download the pack or copy its JSON. You can also open a pack you already have, from its web address or a file, to edit or extend it.

        Open the form

      2. Write a pack by hand

        A pack is a short, plain text file. The format guide explains every field and the rules, and the example pack is ready to copy.

        Format guide Example pack Play the example

      3. Brainstorm with an AI

        Give an AI assistant the format guide and ask for a pack, for example: "Using this format, write a Quartets of You pack of three puzzles about our family's camping trips." Then paste its answer into the form's Open a pack box to check it and try it out. Check the puzzles yourself: AI assistants make mistakes.

      Share your pack

      Put the pack's JSON file online somewhere that allows cross-origin access, so this game can read it from another site. GitHub Pages works, and so does the Raw link of a GitHub gist. Then share this game's address followed by ?pack= and your pack's address.

      For example: https://<this game>/?pack=https://example.org/my-pack.json

      Comfortable with GitHub? Start your own puzzle collection from our template repository. It comes with sample packs, step-by-step hosting on GitHub Pages, and a check that tells you about problems in your packs every time you commit.

      Bravo! All four quartets in one take

      How to play

      • Each puzzle has 16 words that form four hidden quartets of four.
      • Tap four words you think belong together, then tap Submit.
      • A wrong guess is a bum note. Four bum notes end the performance.
      • If three of your four words belong together, you'll see "One note off…".
      • Quartets are named for voices, from B Bass (easiest) through T Tenor and A Alto to S Soprano (hardest).
      • Tutti: before you start, you can sort all sixteen words into four rows and submit once. Get every row right for a Bravo! Any right rows carry over, and play continues as normal.
      • Rehearse mixes up the board. Clear deselects everything.
      • Tap a solved quartet to read its backstory, if it has one.
      • At the end, copy your box score to share: one row per guess, with each word shown as its voice's note, from a plain quarter note ♩ (Bass) to busy sixteenths ♬ (Soprano). It never gives away the words.
